Eleven days.

In the Ituri province of eastern Congo, health workers with no vaccine and no treatment faced an unknown killer. What they did next is older than medicine — and more precise.

Folha de São Paulo / WHO · May 19, 2026

On May 5, health workers in Mongbwalu — a small town in the Ituri province of eastern Congo, near the Ugandan border — noticed something wrong. People were dying, including colleagues. The disease was unknown. They reported it.

On May 15, the National Institute of Biomedical Research in Kinshasa confirmed the cause: the Bundibugyo strain of the Ebola virus. On May 16, the WHO Director-General declared an international public health emergency. On May 19, the WHO Emergency Committee convened in Geneva.

Eleven days from a cluster of unexplained deaths in one of the most remote provinces on earth to a coordinated global response. No vaccine exists for this strain. No specific treatment. The only tool available at every step of that chain was the same: someone saw something, named it, and passed it on.

One hundred and thirty-one people have died. The epidemic is not over.

But something else happened inside this story that the headline didn't say.

The 18th-century mystic known as the Baal Shem Tov — the founder of the Chassidic movement in Eastern Europe — taught that every process of genuine transformation moves through three stages. He called them submission, separation, and sweetening. Each one leads to the next. None can be skipped. And the third — the most mysterious — does not happen without the first two, but it also cannot happen by human effort alone.

The first stage is submission. Not defeat — recognition. The willingness to stop, look directly at what is there, and say: this is real. It is the hardest of the three, because every instinct in a frightened organism pushes toward denial. The health workers of Mongbwalu, watching their colleagues die of something without a name, chose otherwise. They did not negotiate with the reality in front of them. They received it — fully, without flinching — and they reported what they saw. That act, quiet and bureaucratic on the surface, was the first crack in the darkness.

The second stage is separation. Once you have stopped running from what is there, you can begin to distinguish — this from that, cause from symptom, what is known from what is not yet known. This is the ten days of laboratory work in Kinshasa: isolating the virus, identifying the strain, giving it a name. Bundibugyo. Not unknown anymore. The act of naming is never merely semantic. It is the moment at which undifferentiated fear acquires edges — and what has edges can be approached, contained, understood.

The third stage — sweetening — is where the story is still being written. This is the stage the Baal Shem Tov taught cannot be accomplished by human will alone. It requires a partner. But that partnership only becomes available to those who have completed the first two steps. Submission unlocks separation. Separation unlocks something beyond itself — a movement in which human effort and something larger begin to act together. At its most immediate, sweetening looks like containment: the epidemic not spreading beyond Ituri, Uganda, the known borders of the outbreak. At its furthest reach, it looks like a vaccine — the darkness of one hundred and thirty-one deaths converted, over years of research, into protection for millions not yet born.

Neither outcome is guaranteed. The tradition is honest about this. Sweetening is not automatic. It is a gift that becomes available — not before submission, not before separation, but after both. Mongbwalu submitted. Kinshasa separated. Geneva organised the conditions.

What happens next is not entirely in human hands. But it never was. What was in human hands — the recognition, the naming, the transmission — was done. In eleven days. In one of the poorest provinces on earth. By people with no vaccine and no treatment, armed only with the oldest instrument available to our species: the willingness to see what is there and say so.

That is how sweetening begins. Not with a cure. With a report filed in Mongbwalu on May 5th.
